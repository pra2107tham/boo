What Boo actually does
Three existing behaviours are broken or dull. Six new ones give it something to do. Everything here is drawn from the same 64×64 grid the app uses, so these are the real shapes, not sketches.
1 — Typing along with you
New
The one you asked for
Boo puts on little round glasses, sprouts two stubby arms, and a tiny keyboard slides in underneath. Its hands alternate left-right in time with your actual keystroke rate — type fast and it hammers, pause and its hands hover.
How it knows
CGEventSource.secondsSinceLastEventType(.keyDown)
Public API, no accessibility permission, and it reports only when a key was last pressed — never which one. Boo cannot read what you type, by construction.
ENTERS
3+ keystrokes in 2s
LEAVES
4s without a key
Fixing what is broken
The cursor lapFixed
The bug: the orbit set a new target 60×/sec, but each one started a fresh 0.85 s animation that cancelled the last before it moved. It shivered in place instead of lapping.
Fixed: per-frame positions set directly, no animation to cancel. It now genuinely tracks your cursor as it moves.
The focus nudgeRedone
Why it is weird: it flies over and says a random line with no relationship to anything. It nags without knowing whether you were focused.
Redone: only fires after a genuine break in typing during a work stretch, puts a finger to its lips, and says how long you have been going. Earned, not random.
The frightReplaced
Why it is bad: a jump scare is startling once and irritating every time after. It also fires while you are concentrating, which is the worst possible moment.
Replaced with peeking: it hides behind a window edge with one eye and a hand showing, then ducks back. Same mischief, none of the adrenaline.

The rule holding these together
Every behaviour is a reaction to something real, never decoration on a timer. Boo types because you are typing. It shelters because your disk is filling. It shivers because the fans spun up. That is what separates a companion from a screensaver — and it is what the Sphere got right: the viral moment was always reacting to something that just happened.
Restraint
Nine behaviours is plenty to feel alive and far too many to fire often. Each has a long cooldown, none interrupt while you are mid-flow, and every one can be switched off. A pet that performs constantly stops being charming by the second afternoon.
